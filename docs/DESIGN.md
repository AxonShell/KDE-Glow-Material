# Glow Material design principles

## Identity

Glow Material is the MuliOS visual language for KDE. Light is a functional signal: controls illuminate when hovered, focused, selected, or activated. Rounded geometry gives windows and controls a consistent, softer silhouette.

It is not glassmorphism, a permanently neon interface, or a copy of another platform's material system.

## Principles

1. **Dark surfaces first.** Use deep charcoal with enough tonal separation between content, containers, and overlays.
2. **Light communicates interaction.** Resting controls stay quiet. Hover, keyboard focus, selection, and active-window states introduce a visible accent halo.
3. **Rounded geometry.** Window frames and titlebar controls should use consistent rounded forms, including lower window corners.
4. **Layered glow.** Use restrained, layered light around active window edges and interactive controls. Avoid a flat neon outline or a constant pulsing effect.
5. **Purposeful motion.** Transitions clarify state changes; avoid perpetual pulsing and unnecessary movement.
6. **Cross-component consistency.** Plasma, Aurorae, KWin, and Qt integrations share semantic roles while respecting their technical differences.
7. **User control.** Accent color, glow strength, animation, and transparency should be adjustable.

## Initial MuliOS defaults

Deep dark surfaces, MuliOS emerald accent, rounded window geometry, contextual edge and button glow, minimal selective transparency, and short transitions. These defaults are configurable rather than hard-coded limitations.
